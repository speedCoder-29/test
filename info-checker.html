Earthquake
Sudden ground shaking from fault movement
▼
TectonicFault LinesAftershocks
What it is
A sudden release of energy in Earth's crust caused by movement along fault lines, volcanic activity, or tectonic plate collisions. Shaking can last seconds to minutes and may trigger tsunamis, landslides, and fires.
Warning signs
  • Foreshocks (smaller tremors beforehand)
  • Animals behaving unusually or fleeing
  • Radon gas detected in groundwater
  • Ground cracking or tilting (rare, near faults)
During
  • Drop, Cover, Hold On — get under a sturdy table and hold on
  • Stay away from windows, exterior walls, and heavy objects
  • If outdoors, move away from buildings, power lines, and trees
  • If driving, pull over away from overpasses and wait
After
  • Expect aftershocks — some can be nearly as strong as the main quake
  • Check for gas leaks (smell, hissing) — open windows and evacuate if suspected
  • Avoid damaged buildings and downed power lines
  • If near the coast, move to high ground immediately (tsunami risk)
🌊
Tsunami
Massive ocean waves triggered by seabed events
▼
OceanCoastalEarthquake-triggered
What it is
A series of enormous ocean waves caused by an underwater earthquake, volcanic eruption, or submarine landslide. Open-ocean waves travel at up to 800 km/h and are barely noticeable at sea but grow to devastating heights nearshore.
Warning signs
  • Strong or prolonged earthquake felt near the coast
  • Ocean rapidly receding (the "drawback") — exposing the seabed
  • Loud rumbling or roaring sound from the ocean
  • Official tsunami warnings via alert systems
During
  • Move immediately to high ground (30 m+) — do not wait for official confirmation
  • The drawback IS the warning — run, do not stop to watch
  • A building's upper floors may provide refuge if high ground is unreachable
  • Do not return to the coast until authorities declare it safe
After
  • Multiple waves arrive — often the second or third is the largest
  • Wait for the all-clear before returning to coastal areas
  • Avoid floodwater — it is fast-moving and carries debris
  • Beware of sewage contamination in standing water

Hurricane / Typhoon / Cyclone
Rotating tropical storm with extreme winds
▼
WindStorm SurgeFlooding
What it is
A large rotating tropical storm with sustained winds exceeding 119 km/h. The same phenomenon is called a hurricane (Atlantic/eastern Pacific), typhoon (western Pacific), or cyclone (Indian Ocean/Australia). Danger comes from wind, storm surge, and inland flooding.
Warning signs
  • Days-in-advance forecast from meteorological services
  • Rapidly dropping barometric pressure
  • Intensifying rain bands and darkening, thickening clouds
  • Government evacuation orders
During
  • Evacuate storm-surge zones when ordered — surge kills more than wind
  • Shelter in a sturdy building's interior room, away from windows
  • The calm "eye" passing overhead is temporary — the storm returns
  • Avoid flooded roads — 15 cm of water can knock a person over
After
  • Do not go outside until authorities confirm the storm has fully passed
  • Watch for downed power lines and structural damage
  • Avoid floodwaters — they may be contaminated or electrically charged
  • Use generators outdoors only to avoid carbon monoxide poisoning

Tornado
Violent rotating column of air from a thunderstorm
▼
Wind up to 480 km/hBriefDebris
What it is
A rapidly rotating column of air extending from a supercell thunderstorm to the ground. Most last only minutes, but violent EF4–EF5 tornadoes can stay on the ground for over an hour and devastate entire towns.
Warning signs
  • Dark, greenish or purple sky
  • Large hail, often followed by sudden calm
  • Loud continuous roar (like a freight train)
  • Rotating funnel cloud descending from a storm cloud
During
  • Go to the lowest floor, interior room (bathroom, closet, under stairs)
  • Cover yourself with a mattress, blankets, or helmet to protect from debris
  • Mobile homes are unsafe — leave immediately for a sturdy building
  • If caught outdoors with no shelter, lie flat in a low ditch
After
  • Watch for downed power lines and gas leaks
  • Wear sturdy shoes — debris and broken glass everywhere
  • More tornadoes may follow the same storm system
  • Photograph damage before cleanup for insurance

Flood
Overflow of water onto normally dry land
▼
Flash FloodRiver FloodStorm Surge
What it is
Flooding occurs when water overflows onto land that is normally dry. Flash floods develop within 6 hours of heavy rain and are extremely dangerous. River floods develop more slowly but can persist for days. Coastal floods are driven by storm surge or high tides.
Warning signs
  • Heavy or prolonged rainfall upstream
  • Rapidly rising river or stream levels
  • Saturated soil unable to absorb more rain
  • Official flood watches and warnings
During
  • Move to higher ground immediately — never wait
  • Never walk through floodwater: 15 cm can knock you down; 30 cm can move a car
  • Do not drive on flooded roads — "Turn Around, Don't Drown"
  • Disconnect electrical appliances if water is rising indoors
After
  • Avoid floodwater — it is likely contaminated with sewage and chemicals
  • Do not use electrical devices until wiring has been inspected
  • Dry and disinfect all surfaces to prevent mould growth
  • Document all damage with photos before cleanup

Wildfire
Uncontrolled fire spreading through vegetation
▼
Extreme HeatEmbersWind-driven
What it is
An uncontrolled fire that spreads through forests, grasslands, or shrublands, often driven by wind and dry conditions. Wildfires can travel faster than a person can run and produce toxic smoke visible hundreds of kilometres away.
Warning signs
  • Visible smoke on the horizon or smell of burning
  • Ash falling from the sky
  • Sudden strong wind increase in dry conditions
  • Animals fleeing the area
During
  • Evacuate immediately when ordered — early evacuation saves lives
  • If trapped, shelter in a building — close all vents and gaps
  • Cover mouth with a damp cloth; wear long clothing and enclosed shoes
  • Turn on lights so rescue crews can see you through smoke
After
  • Do not return until authorities confirm the area is safe
  • Wear an N95 mask — ash contains toxic particles
  • Watch for "hotspots" — fires can reignite days later
  • Burned slopes are prone to mudslides during rain

Volcanic Eruption
Lava, ash, and gases expelled from a volcano
▼
LavaPyroclastic FlowAsh Cloud
What it is
A volcanic eruption releases lava, ash, toxic gases, and pyroclastic flows (fast-moving currents of hot gas and volcanic matter). Pyroclastic flows can reach 700 km/h and 1000°C. Ash can collapse roofs and contaminate water supplies across vast regions.
Warning signs
  • Increased earthquake swarms near the volcano
  • Ground deformation or swelling detected by sensors
  • Sulfur smell or increased steam venting
  • Changes in volcanic lake colour or temperature
During
  • Evacuate immediately if ordered — pyroclastic flows are unsurvivable
  • Wear an N95 mask and goggles if ash is falling
  • Stay indoors with windows and doors sealed if sheltering
  • Avoid valleys and riverbeds — lahars (volcanic mudflows) travel fast
After
  • Clear ash from roofs — it is very heavy and can cause collapse
  • Do not use tap water until authorities confirm it is safe
  • Wear masks and goggles when cleaning ash
  • Be aware of secondary lahars triggered by rainfall weeks later

Drought
Extended period of abnormally low rainfall
▼
Water ScarcityFood SecurityWildfire Risk
What it is
A drought is an extended period of significantly below-average rainfall that depletes water supplies, reduces crop yields, and raises wildfire risk. Unlike other disasters, droughts develop slowly over months and can persist for years, causing famine and displacement.
Warning signs
  • Consecutive months of below-average rainfall
  • Dropping reservoir, lake, and groundwater levels
  • Dry, cracked soil and yellowing vegetation
  • Government-issued water restrictions
During
  • Follow all water restrictions and conservation orders
  • Fix leaks and replace inefficient fixtures
  • Store drinking water safely if supplies are threatened
  • Be vigilant about fire — drought conditions make ignition easy
After
  • Reintroduce water slowly — sudden flooding of dry soil causes erosion
  • Restore vegetation to prevent long-term soil degradation
  • Assess crop and livestock losses for aid eligibility
  • Plan long-term rainwater harvesting and water storage
❄️
Blizzard / Ice Storm
Severe snow, ice, and wind reducing visibility to near zero
▼
HypothermiaWhite-outIce Accumulation
What it is
A blizzard combines heavy snowfall, sustained winds above 56 km/h, and visibility below 400 metres. Ice storms coat surfaces in freezing rain, making roads impassable and causing power lines and trees to collapse under the weight.
Warning signs
  • Meteorological forecast of blizzard or freezing rain conditions
  • Rapid temperature drop to below freezing with precipitation
  • Darkening skies and strengthening winds
  • Government winter storm warnings
During
  • Stay indoors — wind chill can cause frostbite in minutes
  • Avoid all unnecessary travel; if you must drive, carry an emergency kit
  • Dress in layers (moisture-wicking base, insulating mid, windproof outer)
  • Keep pipes from freezing by letting taps drip slightly
After
  • Clear snow from roofs — heavy accumulation can cause structural collapse
  • Watch for black ice on roads and footpaths
  • Check on elderly and vulnerable neighbours
  • Do not use gas stoves or grills indoors for heating — carbon monoxide risk

Landslide
Mass movement of rock, soil, or debris downslope
▼
Rain-triggeredEarthquake-triggeredMudflow
What it is
A landslide is the movement of rock, earth, or debris down a slope, often triggered by heavy rainfall, earthquakes, or human activity (deforestation, construction). Mudflows (lahars near volcanoes, debris flows) travel at high speed and can bury entire communities.
Warning signs
  • Cracks appearing in ground, roads, or building foundations
  • Tilting or leaning trees, fences, or utility poles
  • New springs or muddy water appearing on slopes
  • Sudden increase in stream turbidity (muddiness)
During
  • Evacuate immediately — landslides move faster than people expect
  • Move away from the slide path to the sides, not upslope
  • If caught, curl into a ball to protect your head and vital organs
  • Avoid river channels during and after heavy rain — debris flows follow them
After
  • Stay away — secondary slides are common within 24–48 hours
  • Check for gas, water, and electrical line damage
  • Report unstable slopes and new ground cracks to authorities
  • Burned slopes are especially prone to slides during subsequent rainfall
⚡
Lightning / Severe Thunderstorm
Electrical discharge, hail, strong winds, and heavy rain
▼
HailElectricFlash Flood
What it is
A thunderstorm produces lightning, thunder, heavy rain, and often hail or strong winds. Lightning strikes kill more people globally each year than tornadoes or hurricanes, and severe thunderstorms are a leading cause of flash floods and power outages.
Warning signs
  • Darkening skies and distant rumbling thunder
  • Hair standing on end or skin tingling (lightning is about to strike nearby)
  • Sudden drop in temperature and wind shift
  • Thunder within 30 seconds of a lightning flash = storm is within 10 km
During
  • Seek shelter in a substantial building or metal-roofed vehicle
  • 30-30 rule: If thunder follows lightning in under 30 s, shelter immediately; wait 30 min after the last thunder before going out
  • Avoid tall trees, open fields, hilltops, water, and metal objects
  • If caught outdoors with no shelter, crouch low with feet together — do not lie flat
After
  • Check for lightning-strike fires on your property (roof, trees)
  • Do not touch a lightning strike victim — they carry no residual charge and need CPR
  • Watch for flash flooding from the storm's heavy rain
  • Surge-protect electronics or unplug during storms
🥵
Heatwave
Prolonged extreme heat — the world's deadliest weather event
▼
Heat StrokeDehydrationWildfire Risk
What it is
A heatwave is a prolonged period of abnormally high temperatures, often combined with high humidity. Heatwaves kill more people worldwide each year than any other weather-related disaster. The elderly, young children, and outdoor workers are most vulnerable.
Warning signs
  • Forecast of temperatures significantly above seasonal averages for 3+ days
  • High overnight temperatures not allowing the body to recover
  • Government heat health alerts or emergency declarations
During
  • Stay in air-conditioned spaces during the hottest part of the day (10am–4pm)
  • Drink water frequently — do not wait until you feel thirsty
  • Wear light, loose, light-coloured clothing; use sunscreen
  • Check on elderly neighbours, young children, and pets regularly
  • Heat stroke signs: hot/dry skin, confusion, rapid pulse — call emergency services immediately
After
  • Cool down gradually — sudden cold water can cause shock
  • Rehydrate with water and electrolytes, not alcohol or caffeine
  • Assess and report any heat-related illnesses to health authorities
  • Review and improve home insulation for future heatwaves
